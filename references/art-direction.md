# Culturally grounded art direction

Use this menu after the display geometry is known. Present one decision level at a time. The goal is not merely an attractive image: every direction must belong to a recognizable visual tradition and carry an idea that changes the composition.

## Non-negotiable direction contract

Before proposing a scene, establish three compatible anchors:

1. **Artistic lineage** — a historical movement, period, medium, design school, cinematic grammar, or public-domain compositional reference.
2. **Philosophical or literary premise** — an idea expressed by a visible relation, contradiction, ritual, scale difference, repetition, absence, or transformation.
3. **Material grammar** — the image's physical-looking construction: lens and film stock, oil glaze, engraving, halftone, torn paper, ink wash, textile, stained glass, or another specific surface language.

Do not stack references arbitrarily. Use one dominant artistic lineage, at most one supporting lineage, and one philosophical premise. Translate every reference into concrete visual decisions instead of leaving it as a name.

When a known work is used, treat it as a conceptual or compositional anchor rather than an image to copy. Prefer public-domain works and museum-held historical references. If the user's theme does not fit the examples below, identify a more relevant historical work or tradition instead of forcing a fashionable label.

## Excluded generic-AI shortcuts

Do not offer these as standalone styles:

- epic fantasy realism;
- painterly concept art;
- generic anime key visual;
- high-end 3D render;
- cinematic AI art;
- science-fiction realism without a design lineage;
- magical portal, hooded hero, monumental castle, or glowing runes as automatic fantasy defaults;
- cyan-and-orange or cyan-on-black grading without a conceptual reason;
- volumetric fog, sparks, floating particles, glossy armor, and excessive micro-detail used only to simulate production value.

These elements may appear only when the user explicitly requests them and the direction contract gives them a cultural or narrative function. A fantasy subject is valid; generic game-store key art is not.

## Preferred generation modes

Favor directions where a multimodal image model can follow clear spatial and material rules:

- documentary, architectural, noir, or staged photography with a specific lens, light source, and framing;
- editorial illustration built around one readable visual metaphor;
- limited-palette modernist posters and geometric systems;
- printmaking, halftone, grain, stippling, collage, paper, textile, and other tactile surfaces;
- surreal scenes governed by one explicit impossible rule inside an otherwise coherent world;
- restrained minimal or atmospheric compositions with strong negative space;
- historically grounded fantasy or science fiction whose architecture, costume, and medium follow one chosen lineage.

Prefer a small number of well-bound objects and relations over ornamental abundance. Use multi-turn edits to correct one visual property at a time, and use supplied reference images for composition or material when available.

## Canonical reference anchors

Offer only anchors relevant to the chosen subject:

- **Caspar David Friedrich, _Wanderer above the Sea of Fog_** — human scale against the sublime; use the scale relation and contemplative viewpoint, not the exact figure or landscape.
- **Giovanni Battista Piranesi, _Carceri d'invenzione_** — systems, impossible architecture, bureaucracy, and entrapment through repeated stairs and vast interiors.
- **Francisco de Goya, _The Sleep of Reason Produces Monsters_** — reason producing its own nightmare; use the reversal between rational order and irrational consequence.
- **Pieter Bruegel the Elder, _The Tower of Babel_** — collective ambition, administrative complexity, and Promethean hubris expressed through monumental construction.
- **Katsushika Hokusai, _The Great Wave off Kanagawa_** — rhythmic contour, cropped viewpoint, small human craft against a dominant natural form.
- **Arnold Böcklin, _Isle of the Dead_** — memory, silence, passage, and compressed symbolic landscape.
- **Honoré Daumier's lithographic satire** — social types, institutional absurdity, economical caricature, and print texture.
- **Hieronymus Bosch's triptychs** — distributed moral narrative and accumulative consequence; use only when dense multiplicity serves the concept.

Do not make the user choose from this list unless the works genuinely fit. Explain in one sentence what visual principle each proposed anchor contributes.

## Five grounded families

### 1. Photography and authored cinema

- **Documentary or social observation** — natural light, ordinary gestures, regional specificity, imperfect framing.
- **Film noir and expressionist cinema** — hard shadow geometry, moral ambiguity, compressed urban space.
- **French New Wave grammar** — available light, off-center framing, lived-in streets, visual ellipsis.
- **Staged absurdist tableau** — believable photography arranged around one impossible social ritual.
- **Architectural photography** — rigorous perspective, material truth, human scale against systems.

Recommend this family for contemporary subjects, satire, cities, or emotional realism. Name the photographic or cinematic grammar rather than saying only `cinematic`.

### 2. Painting, drawing, and print history

- **Romantic sublime** — overwhelming nature, fragile human scale, turbulent light.
- **Symbolism** — recurring objects, dream logic, moral or spiritual metaphor.
- **Surrealism or metaphysical painting** — ordinary spaces made uncanny through impossible relations and stillness.
- **Expressionism or social realism** — compressed perspective, emotional distortion, labor and social structure.
- **Ukiyo-e and woodblock traditions** — flat depth, rhythmic contour, cropped viewpoints, weather as structure.
- **Illuminated manuscript or historical engraving** — hierarchical scale, marginal scenes, dense narrative pattern.

Recommend this family when the user wants an image that feels authored and historically legible. Describe the movement's compositional rules; do not request a copy of a specific protected artwork.

### 3. Modern graphic and editorial traditions

- **Bauhaus or Constructivism** — functional geometry, diagonal energy, limited palette, social machinery.
- **Art Deco or Neo Deco** — stepped symmetry, metallic rhythm, monumental ornament.
- **Swiss and International design** — grid discipline, negative space, reduction, strong scale relationships without mandatory text.
- **Op Art or geometric abstraction** — optical rhythm and continuous structures that cross seams cleanly.
- **Mid-century editorial illustration** — simplified shapes, controlled palette, visual metaphor, strong silhouette.
- **Editorial surrealism** — one clear conceptual impossibility rendered with graphic economy.

Recommend this family for clean continuity, political or social metaphor, icon-heavy desktops, and large physical gaps.

### 4. Printmaking, collage, and tactile craft

- **Linocut or woodcut** — carved contrast, directional marks, monumental silhouettes.
- **Risograph or screen print** — limited inks, visible registration, halftone, paper grain.
- **Dada, photomontage, or zine collage** — cut edges, scale collisions, found-material energy.
- **Textile, tapestry, or embroidery** — repeating motifs, woven flow, tactile continuity.
- **Stained glass, mosaic, or ceramic relief** — modular color, luminous boundaries, durable large shapes.

Recommend this family when source resolution is constrained: material texture and graphic shapes survive resampling better than synthetic micro-detail. Keep imperfections intentional and coherent.

### 5. Minimal, atmospheric, and contemplative traditions

- **Minimalism or color-field composition** — few large relationships, restrained palette, contemplative scale.
- **Ink wash or sumi-e inspired** — emptiness, gestural flow, atmospheric depth.
- **Silhouette and cut-paper landscape** — layered planes, strong readability, seam tolerance.
- **Monochrome engraving** — value structure, line density, historical atmosphere.
- **Liminal architectural image** — quiet repetition, absence, threshold spaces, uncanny restraint.

Recommend this family for irregular arrangements, very large gaps, calm desktops, or the highest probability of clean continuity.

## Philosophical and literary lenses

Offer only two or three that fit the user's subject. Translate each into a visible device:

- **The sublime** — a small human measure confronted with an overwhelming system or landscape.
- **Absurdism** — a precise ritual repeated despite its obvious futility; the contradiction should be visible.
- **Existential choice** — a figure, threshold, or fork where empty space makes responsibility palpable.
- **Alienation** — people, objects, or homes separated from the labor or meaning that produced them.
- **Panopticism and discipline** — radial sightlines, repeated cells, observation architecture, self-policing bodies.
- **The spectacle** — images and staging replacing lived reality; crowds face the representation rather than the world.
- **Simulacra** — copies recursively displacing the absent original.
- **Impermanence and memory** — erosion, palimpsest, seasonal passage, repair, traces, or repeated absence.
- **Utopia revealing dystopia** — perfect order whose hidden cost is visible in a second layer or neighboring screen.
- **Promethean hubris** — a gift of power that visibly exceeds its maker's control.

Do not place philosophy in the prompt as a decorative proper noun. State how it changes scale, staging, repetition, viewpoint, or cause and effect.

## Reliable pairings

Use these as patterns, not fixed recipes:

- Bureaucratic dystopia: Constructivist geometry + absurdism or panopticism + screen-print texture.
- Consumer satire: editorial surrealism + spectacle or simulacra + staged photographic realism.
- Fantasy: Romantic sublime or illuminated-manuscript logic + Promethean hubris + engraving or oil-glaze material.
- Science fiction: Bauhaus, Suprematist, or Art Deco design logic + cosmic sublime + precise architectural surfaces.
- Nature: Ukiyo-e rhythm or ink wash + impermanence + paper and pigment texture.
- Urban melancholy: expressionist cinema + alienation + grainy monochrome photography.
- Dream or memory: Symbolism + palimpsest + layered translucent paint or collage.

## Cross-screen interaction patterns

Fit the interaction to both the geometry and the premise:

- **Cause and effect:** one screen initiates a visible transformation completed on another.
- **Shared journey:** a road, river, procession, rail, shadow, or visual rhythm crosses the displays.
- **Tether:** a rope, cable, branch, ribbon, beam, smoke trail, or gaze links distinct subjects.
- **Opposition:** two forces face each other across a gap, with the empty space carrying tension.
- **Reveal:** one display contains the apparent order; another reveals its mechanism, memory, or cost.
- **Continuous world:** one horizon and light field unify separate vignettes.

For three screens, prefer origin, bridge, and consequence. Make the bridge carry the philosophical idea, not only decorative motion.

## Prompt construction

Build the art block in this order:

```text
Subject and action: <what visibly happens>
Artistic lineage: <movement or tradition> translated into <composition, palette, shape, perspective>
Philosophical premise: <idea> made visible through <specific relation or metaphor>
Material grammar: <physical medium, texture, imperfection, light behavior>
Cross-screen structure: <shared plane, anchor positions, direction, gap behavior>
Avoid: <generic AI tropes that would flatten this direction>
```

Prefer concrete language. `A diagonal procession of identical desks narrows toward a watchtower` is stronger than `a Foucauldian dystopia`. `Visible halftone dots, two misregistered inks, rough uncoated paper` is stronger than `artistic poster style`.

## Anti-generic review

Reject or rewrite a direction when any answer is `yes`:

- Would removing the artistic reference leave essentially the same image?
- Is the philosophy merely named rather than visible in the staging?
- Could the result pass as generic game key art, wallpaper-site fantasy, or a stock AI splash screen?
- Does spectacle come mainly from cyan glow, particles, fog, impossible mountains, or ornamental micro-detail?
- Are several incompatible styles being blended without hierarchy?
- Is the palette the model's default blue-orange contrast rather than a deliberate color script?
- Is every surface digitally smooth, with no medium-specific texture or imperfection?

Before generation, summarize the final direction in one sentence containing the lineage, premise, and medium. If that sentence is vague, return to art direction instead of generating.

## Composition rules

- Keep the dominant horizon or structural rhythm continuous in art-space, not merely at the same output-pixel row.
- Cross seams with large, simple shapes; do not rely on fingers, faces, typography, or tiny particles.
- Preserve one lighting direction and one intentional color script across the entire master.
- Treat physical gaps as invisible missing world space.
- Reserve calm negative space for icons, docks, widgets, and menu bars.
- Avoid symmetrical duplication unless it serves the concept.
- Do not include logos or recognizable copyrighted characters unless the user explicitly requests them and the image policy permits the request.
